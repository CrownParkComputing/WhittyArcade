The arcade wall

Several cabinets. One screen. All of them awake.

Pick a handful of games and WhittyArcade puts them side by side across the display. They are not videos or thumbnails: every one is a live cabinet running its own attract mode, waiting for a coin. How many you can have is worked out from your machine, so a big PC on a wide monitor gets a bigger wall.

The one you are on

Grows to fill the middle

Whichever cabinet has your attention takes the biggest share of the wall, with the rest dividing what is left. Move across to a neighbour and the wall re-lays itself around your new game.

Sound

Only the game you are playing

The rest keep running silently, so a whole wall sounds like one cabinet rather than an amusement arcade at closing time.

Swap

Change one, keep the others

Bored of a column? Open the shelf from inside the wall and put a different game in its place. The cabinets either side never stop.

Cabinet art

Framed like the real thing

Games can be surrounded by their arcade cabinet's bezel artwork, fetched once for the games you own and kept on your machine afterwards. The picture is fitted into the artwork's screen opening rather than pasted over it.

Why bother

Because it looks tremendous

Put three racers up at once and let them attract at each other. Or park a game you love next to one you have never played, and see which one you reach for.

Two players

Five ways to get a second person in

Choose a game and WhittyArcade asks how you want to play it, offering only what that particular board can actually do. A one-player cabinet just starts.

Take turns on this cabinetTwo players, one screen, the original alternating game. Galaga, Galaxian, Moon Cresta, UniWar S, Phoenix, Pac-Mania, Shinobi and Ghosts'n Goblins.
Both at once on this cabinetTwo sets of controls live at the same time, for the games built that way — Virtua Cop and Virtua Cop 2.
A screen eachTwo player views instead of one: split down a single wide monitor, or one monitor each when you have a second display. Each view keeps the cabinet's real proportions with a border round it rather than being stretched.
Across two computersOpen WhittyArcade on both machines. They find each other, sort out who is Player 1, and only offer the games you both have installed. Player 1 picks; Player 2's copy launches itself.
Linked cabinetsSega Rally Championship, Ridge Racer 2 and Rave Racer race through the link hardware their real cabinets used — two whole boards talking to each other, either side by side on one PC or one on each of two.

Galaga, a view each across one ultrawide display

Across two computers

No homework first

Nothing to configure. No IP address, no host-or-join, no port numbers, no deciding who is cabinet one. Two copies on the same network see each other and say so, and the games list narrows to what you both own.

While you play

Two whole machines, in step

Both computers run the entire arcade board and pass only the controls between them, so each screen draws at full speed with its own sound. If the two ever drift apart, the game says so on screen instead of quietly leaving you playing different matches.

The front end

Built to be used from the sofa

Everything is reachable with a joypad: the shelf, the sorting, the settings, the pause menu. The keyboard is there if you want it, not because you need it.

A wall of cover art

Every game is a card carrying its own arcade title screen. Nothing but the artwork, no caption repeating what the picture already says.

Flip through the hardware

Turn the shelf into a page per arcade board, or a page per publisher — Namco, Sega, Capcom, Irem, Nichibutsu, Amstar. Push past the end of a page to turn it, and an info button tells you what the board actually was.

Sort it your way

A to Z, most played, or whatever you added most recently. There is also a filter for how you want to play, so a two-player night only shows games that can do it.

Hall of Fame

An arcade ranking screen where the rows sweep in one at a time and the champion's medal pulses. Real scores, read out of the games themselves — Phoenix, Moon Cresta, Shinobi and Ghosts'n Goblins so far.

Operator's keys

Pause any game and the cabinet's own switches are right there: coinage, lives, difficulty, and the rest of the settings the arcade owner used to open the coin door for.

Your controls, your way

Bind anything to anything, globally, per board, or per game. Pads are remembered by device, so plugging the same one back in brings its settings with it.

Getting going

Point it at your games and go

Bring your own ROMs

WhittyArcade ships no game data and links to none. On first run it asks where your collection already lives, then reads it where it sits — nothing is imported, copied, extracted or repacked, and you can change the folder later.

Standard MAME archives and folders are read as they are, and a built-in audit tells you which of your sets are ready to play.

Download or build

Builds for Windows and Linux, plus what each one needs to run, are on the downloads page. Nothing to install: unpack it and run it.

If you would rather compile it yourself, the source and build instructions are on GitHub.

Under the hood: each board is emulated in full — original processors, video and sound hardware — and drawn straight to the screen through OpenGL or Vulkan on Windows and Linux.
